In this post, I want to share why I decided to take the [Google UX Design Professional Certificate](https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/google-ux-design) and what I hope to get out of it.

## Why UX? Why Now?

For a long time, I used to joke: "I can build apps that run on a black and white screen and write boring files." That was enough to land good jobs and work on cool projects. But let's be honest, being so limited sucks. Sure, I can spin up a backend, but if no one can use it, what's the point? Time to change that.

So, what's my goal?

> Design and prototype at least one complete digital product (website or app) that solves a real problem, applying UX principles learned from the course, and share the process and results publicly.

I have already completed the [Web Design for Everybody specialization on Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/web-design), where I learned HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, and Responsive Design. While that course was excellent for building websites, it didn't cover design principles or user experience. Also, I'm not looking to learn frameworks like [React](https://react.dev/) or [Vue](https://vuejs.org/) at this stage because  those are "implementation details" that I can pick up as needed. What I truly need is the foundational ability to design products in the first place.

## My Frontend Experiments

I have played around with frontend development before:

- Built the [first version of my personal website from scratch](https://github.com/lucasguesserts/lucasguesserts.github.io?at=790213b821232dab4563a7ba7dd922466645b56a) using only what I learned in the Web Design course.
- Developed the [Packing Visualizer](https://github.com/lucasguesserts/packing-visualizer) to visualize the results of my master's algorithm.
- Forked and tweaked [Show do Milhão](https://lucasguesserts.github.io/show-do-milhao/) ([git repository](https://github.com/lucasguesserts/show-do-milhao)) so I could play it with my wife's family (they love the show).

All fun projects, but honestly, I never really focused on UX. It just wasn't my priority, and I didn't have the experience.

That's about to change.

## Why This Course?

I did some research and found [Best Coursera UX/UI Design Certificates in 2026: Google vs IBM vs Microsoft](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/best-coursera-ux-ui-design-certificates/) by The Interview Guys. I wanted a course that was deep enough to be useful, but not so long or specialized that I'd lose interest. I also wanted something that could help my career.

IBM's course? Too long, with too much focus on user research (interesting, but not what I need right now). Microsoft's? Too focused on Microsoft tools (I don't use them and don't plan to). Google's course felt just right: not too short, not too long, not too detailed, not too shallow. Plus, it looks great on a resume.

## What's Next?

I'm not sure what I'll build with my new skills yet, but I'm excited to find out.
