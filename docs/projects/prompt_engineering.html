Project: Prompt Engineering

Motivation

Everybody is talking about AI, vibe-coding, coding AI, bla bla bla. Although I don't see the extraordinary evidence to support to AI taking over the world (in every imaginable sense), I still think AI - talking to a chatbot - has its use.

Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence - Carl Sagan.

AI is a tool, as any other humans have developed. I like to compare it with a calculator. Have calculators take the job of mathematicians, staticicians, or engineers? Not really, we just adapted to use those tools to develop more complex and refined projects. That is what I see happening with AI.

As any other tool, we must learn how to use it properly. To that end, I decided to take the course "Prompt Engineering for ChatGPT" of professor Jules White.

The Mindset for using AI

  • the AI is a tool to expand our capabilities.
  • interact with the AI so to give a form to your thoughs and iterate through ideas quickly.
  • tasks that used to take hours before - searching for information, creating a proof-of-concept code - can now be done in minutes. Use it to explore and experiment more.
  • Think about conversations and iterations (a conversation is about sharing information and understanding each other; it is also about refining common understanding).
  • LLMs have a randomized answer. Use it in your favor, to explore and experiment more.
  • add specificity to control your prompts. Vague prompt → Vague answer. Specific prompt → Specific answer. Specific prompts make it break out of the training data.
  • always give enough context and data to the LLM.

Prompt Engineering Principles

  • A prompt sets the context for the conversation and tells the LLM what information is important and what the desired output form and content should be.
  • Prompt engineering is the means by which LLMs are programmed via prompts.
  • A prompt is a set of instructions provided to an LLM that programs the LLM by customizing it and/or enhancing or refining its capabilities.
  • The quality of the output(s) generated by a conversational LLM is directly related to the quality of the prompts provided by the user.

Side Effects

I have the feeling that those are not patters only for LLMs but for human interaction too. I mean, I could easily use some of those techniques with my interactions with other people. The flipped interaction patter, for instance, I can use when talking to an expert so I can provide a basic context and I let they drive the conversation. I can also use myself, when people start telling me something and I switch to asking clarifying questions.

I have looked for references on how to make good questions (I was interested in scientific research). The best reference is "How to solve it", which provides a series of heuristics. The way I see now, I could use some techniques of Prompt Engineering to do just that.

There is another aspect, which is talking to people. Things like "always provide enough context" seem reasonable also when talking to humans. We always assume people know some things (those basic things no one can agree on what is), but seldom is the case in which the other person really has the context we want them to have to talk/answer us.
