This story contains references of nice personal websites, with an analysis of their focus.

## [Mateus Felipe Colla](https://mateusfcolla.github.io/)

It is a single-page website with all the information required.

I like:

1. it is a single page;
2. links open in a new tab, so people do not lose his site;
3. a nice and brief description of himself and works he did in the past;
4. tech stack using icons;
5. clear message of what he offers;
6. a summary on top that does not vanishes, so people can reach other parts of the page easily;
7. it has many nice images;
8. nice title and icon in the tab bar;

I don't like:

1. things moving;
2. broken links;

## [Coding Horror](https://blog.codinghorror.com/)

It is a blog, a place for him to put whatever he wants.

I like:

1. a clean page, with the text in a readable and comfortable width;

I don't like:

1. the posts are all expanded, it is difficult to navigate through them. I must point out that it is a feature, because then the reader doesn't have to click, they just start reading;

## [Dhole](https://soatok.blog/b/)

I like:

1. the colors make it easy to read;
2. the width of the text is nice, so is the font and the spacing;
3. there are plenty of figures, so the text doesn't seem so dense (when it actually is);

I don't like:

1. where do I start? There is no content to read, nothing intuitive to easily guide me to where I want to go;
2. too many figures, too many things, it is kinda overwhelming;

## [Jean de Dieu](https://jeandedieu.vercel.app/)

I love this one. Though the guy does not have much experience, it is the kind of thing I am looking for. It is simple, minimalistic, clear.

I like:

1. it has minimal information, all one needs to see and know (he does not have much experience);
2. his picture is round, it looks much better;

I don't like:

1. the font and size make it difficult to read;
